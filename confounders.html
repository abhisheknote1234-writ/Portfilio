Discovering Confounding Variables

To be reliable, health trackers need multiple sensors working together.

01 · Overview

The Problem: Algorithms Breaking in Real Life

In controlled lab experiments, everything is tidy: room temperature is set, people sleep well, and they stay completely still. But in real everyday life, our bodies are constantly changing. When I tested heart-monitoring algorithms outside the lab, the models made huge mistakes.

If someone only slept 4 hours, their heart patterns looked identical to someone experiencing severe psychological panic. And if someone had a mild fever starting, their faster heart rate was misclassified as "high emotional excitement"—even while they were resting quietly in bed.

02 · The Core Question

What was I trying to understand?

What everyday factors (like lack of sleep, mild sickness, or simply standing up) fool heart sensors? And can a single heart sensor ever reliably tell the difference between mental stress and physical strain without other sensors to help it?

03 · Hidden Traps Comparison

Real-World Factors That Fool Heart Sensors

Lack of Sleep (4h vs 8h) −34% Heart Rate Variability drop Misclassified as "Severe Anxiety" or "High Stress" even while calmly reading.

05 · What The Data Showed

Breaking Down the Numbers

Hidden Trap 01 · Lack of Sleep
−34% Variability

Lack of sleep prevents the heart from relaxing properly. Heart rate variability dropped from a healthy 44 ms down to 29 ms. A basic heart sensor algorithm thought the person was in extreme panic, even while they were calmly relaxing.

Hidden Trap 02 · Mild Fever
+14 bpm Jump

When fighting a mild sickness, the body speeds up the heart to circulate immune cells. Just a 0.9°C temperature rise pushed resting heart rate up by 14 beats per minute. Simple AI models thought this was intense emotional arousal.

06 · The Reality Check

Why Single Sensors Fail in the Real World

The big mistake many algorithms make is assuming the heart only beats faster when you're emotional or stressed. But the heart is a working muscle: its first job is pumping oxygen, regulating blood pressure, and fighting infection.

The Single-Sensor Problem: Without knowing if someone is walking, running a fever, or running on 3 hours of sleep, a single heart sensor cannot tell the difference between an emotional argument, a cold starting, or walking up a flight of stairs.
07 · Key Takeaway

What I Learned: Combining Sensors is Mandatory

This was the major turning point of my research. I realized that clever software alone cannot fix a missing sensor. If a device only has one sensor, it's operating half-blind.

To make health AI truly reliable, the physical hardware must combine:

  • Optical pulse (PPG): To see how fast blood travels from the heart to the fingers.
  • Precision skin temperature: To instantly catch fevers and sleep recovery.
  • Movement sensor (IMU): To filter out walking and standing before checking for stress.
08 · Limitations

What to Keep in Mind

  • Sleep data was tested across consecutive nights rather than an overnight hospital sleep lab.
  • The fever data came from a natural mild cold rather than a clinical medical trial.
