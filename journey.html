Phase 01 · Exploration & Audit DREAMER Dataset

Emotion Recognition via EEG & ECG

I ran a rigorous temporal audit across 14 training subjects (252 trials, 18 stimuli) evaluating 30s trajectories and snapshot windows. While physiology models hovered at median balanced accuracies of 0.426–0.576, the clip-only baseline reached 0.828 for Valence and 0.755 for Dominance.

Multiplicity correction eliminated all apparent regional effects (0 of 1,242 comparisons survived Holm adjustment). The classifiers were not decoding universal emotional signatures—they were fitting stimulus-specific artifacts.

Observation

Phase 02 · Counter Solution Hardware Acquisition · ECG + PPG

DIY ECG & PPG Monitoring System

Instead of relying on ambiguous labels from strangers, I built my own synchronous biopotential (ECG) and optical pulse (PPG) acquisition rig to study my own cardiac dynamics and pulse transit under controlled conditions.

Designing the analog front-end and optical photoplethysmography interface taught me that biopotential signal integrity at the skin interface matters far more than downstream software filtering.

📓 Field Note Phase 02
Hardware Front-End Log
Initial Assumption

Software filters and wavelets can clean up raw sensor noise later in Python, so breadboard analog circuitry doesn't need to be perfect.

What Hardware Revealed

When I hooked up electrodes and optical sensors on a breadboard, hand motion and ground loops saturated the ADC instantly. Once an analog signal clips, no algorithm can recover the real pulse waveform.

📓 Field Note Phase 03
Continuous Self-Tracking
Initial Assumption

Textbooks define resting heart rate as 60–100 BPM and high HRV as calm. A static threshold should easily detect stress states.

What Self-Tracking Revealed

Tracking my own body over weeks, my resting baseline shifted by 25 BPM based on sleep debt and digestion. Fixed thresholds labeled me "stressed" right after eating lunch. Wearable baselines must be personalized.

Phase 04 · Cognitive Validation OpenNeuro ds006848 · N=30

Question-Level Cardiac Dynamics in Working Memory

Audited 5,959 Sternberg memory trials with continuous 1000 Hz ECG across 30 participants. Discovered a reproducible evoked deceleration (−1.93 BPM) during mental holding, and verified that attenuated decelerators suffered a selective −8.32% accuracy deficit under speed pressure (p = 0.0084).

Ran an adversarial forensic audit with False Discovery Rate and Linear Mixed Models to eliminate short-window mathematical artifacts and prove statistical authenticity.

Observation

"Cardiac deceleration is not noise—it is a functional physiological correlate of mental resource allocation under cognitive load."

📓 Field Note Phase 04
Working Memory Dynamics
Initial Assumption

Working memory decisions happen in fractions of a second, so slower heart rhythms probably wouldn't show clear question-level patterns.

What 5,959 Trials Revealed

Across 30 people, heart rate consistently dropped by -1.93 BPM while holding items in mind. People whose heart failed to slow made 8.3% more mistakes when rushed for time.

Phase 05 · Hitting Limits Contextual Factors

Discovering Confounding Variables

Taking models out of controlled protocols and into daily routines exposed major confounders: acute sleep loss suppressed RMSSD by 34%, and subclinical illness produced a +14 BPM resting tachycardia shift without any emotional trigger.

A single-sensor ECG algorithm cannot distinguish cognitive stress from physical fatigue without sleep, temperature, and motion context.

Observation

"Autonomic signals cannot be decoded in a vacuum. Context is the difference between real mental stress and a poor night's sleep."

Sleep Loss: −34% RMSSD Febrile Drift: +14 BPM Circadian Baseline Sensitivity

📓 Field Note Phase 05
Real-World Confounders
Initial Assumption

Once heart rate models work well in controlled tests, they are ready to package into a real-time focus monitor app.

What Daily Life Revealed

One night of poor sleep cut my HRV by 34%, and a minor cold raised resting heart rate by +14 BPM with zero mental stress. Without tracking movement, sleep, and body temperature together, standalone heart metrics give constant false alarms.

📓 Field Note Phase 06
Longitudinal Data Audit
Initial Assumption

Automated feature engineering will smoothly extract continuous cycle trends from raw smartwatch sensor streams.

What Data Audit Revealed

Smartwatch data had ~45% missing periods due to charging and daily routines. When look-back windows collapsed on short wear segments, noise was artificially amplified. Fixing these pipeline issues established a realistic, robust separation of Cohen's d = 0.27.
