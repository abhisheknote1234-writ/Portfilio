01 · Overview

Physiological Modeling in Real-World Health Data

In consumer health tech, analyzing real-world biosignals requires rigorous data auditing. Across multi-month health ring and smartwatch recordings from 42 women, I analyzed heart rate, heart rate variability, and skin temperature to isolate true biological trends.

My audit verified that cycle phase variation represents a realistic, subtle shift of Cohen's d = 0.27, while uncovering a reliable early warning dip in nighttime heart variability prior to cramp onset.

02 · The Big Question

What was I trying to understand?

Can longitudinal wearable health trackers detect genuine hormonal and autonomic shifts across natural menstrual cycles, and can nighttime biosignals anticipate physical pain symptoms before they start?

⚡
The Focus: Separating genuine biological signals from wearable recording artifacts and battery gaps to establish trustworthy health markers.
03 · Data Quality Audit

Quantifying Longitudinal Missingness

A baseline availability audit across 42 participants revealed substantial gaps in continuous commercial wearable coverage:

Physiological Stream Overall Coverage (%) Missingness Profile Forensic Implication
Heart Rate (HR) 68.2% Periodic daytime dropouts, charging intervals Acceptable for rolling hourly baselines
Heart Rate Variability (HRV) 45.8% Extreme nocturnal skew, daytime motion rejection Cannot support continuous real-time classification
Skin Temperature (Temp) 52.1% Intermittent contact loss, ambient sensor resets Requires baseline calibration per wear segment
Symptom Logs (Cramps/Pain) Sparse Subjective recall delay (1–12 hours) Ground-truth timestamps require fuzzy onset windows

With HRV coverage at only 45.8%, any algorithm claiming to continuously forecast cycle transitions 24/7 in real time was mathematically unfeasible without imputing more than half the autonomic data stream.

04 · Discovered Pipeline Bugs

Two Critical Architectural Pipeline Flaws

Diving into individual sample traces revealed two distinct software bugs that completely invalidated earlier published results:

BUG 01
Look-Back Window Truncation
To prevent across-session leakage, look-back windows were bounded to the current wear segment start (bounded_since = max(since, t_start)). Because the longest continuous wear segment was 31.25 hours, the DAILY (24h), WEEKLY (7d), and MONTHLY (30d) features were bounded to the exact same raw samples. As a result, daily, weekly, and monthly features were mathematically identical (correlation 1.000).
BUG 02
Noise Amplification by StandardScaler
Because daily and weekly means were identical, their difference feature was pure floating-point noise (std ≈ 10-6). When passed into Scikit-Learn's StandardScaler, dividing by 0.000003 amplified micro-precision numerical jitter by over 300,000x to force a unit variance of 1.0, turning numerical noise into the primary feature driving cluster separation.
05 · Statistical Validation

Validated Effect Size: Cohen's d = 0.27

Across all 42 participants, statistical modeling of heart rate and autonomic variation between follicular and luteal phases yields:

📊
Validated Separation: Cohen's d = 0.27 across longitudinal cycle phases.

An effect size of 0.27 reflects a subtle, natural physiological shift across menstrual phases. This aligns with clinical literature on autonomic variation, confirming that physiological changes in consumer wearables are real but require multi-day tracking rather than single-day assumptions.

06 · Real Biological Finding

Primary Biological Finding: Early Cramp Warning

Across the longitudinal recordings, one genuine physical pattern proved consistent:

Early Physical Warning Before Cramps (HRV & Heart Rate)
Real Biological Pattern
Early Warning Signal: 24 to 36 hours before people reported painful cramps, their nighttime heart rate variability showed a distinct dip followed by an increase in resting heart rate. The body's nervous system senses inflammation coming a full day in advance.

Unlike the broken mathematical features, this early warning signal survived all verification tests, showing genuine potential for early alerts before physical pain starts.

07 · Key Takeaways

What this experience taught me

"I stopped asking whether the machine learning model worked — and started asking whether the data was trustworthy in the first place."

1. If Numbers Look Too Good to Be True, They Are: When an algorithm claims an impossibly high score, don't celebrate—check your code for bugs and data leakage.

2. Missing Data is Normal: People take off smartwatches to shower or charge them. If your software assumes 24/7 continuous perfection, it will break.

3. Auditing Builds Better Devices: Rigorous data verification and uncovering genuine biological baselines is what real engineering is about. These findings directly shaped my next wearable platform, Novara.
